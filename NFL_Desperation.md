NFL DESPERATION

Official Rules — Version 1.0

1. The Game

NFL Desperation is a season-long NFL prediction game built around one simple question:

How desperate are you willing to get?

Each week, players select the teams they believe will win NFL games. The more games you correctly select, the more points you can earn.

There is, however, a catch:

One wrong pick can turn an otherwise perfect week into zero points.

The game rewards both accuracy and nerve.

2. Eligible Games

NFL Desperation uses NFL regular-season games only.

No preseason games.
No playoff games.
Every NFL contest scheduled during a Desperation week is eligible.
This includes Thursday, Sunday, Monday, Saturday, international, and other specially scheduled games.
The game follows the actual NFL schedule, including unusual scheduling and flexed games.
A Desperation week generally begins with the first scheduled NFL contest on Thursday and concludes when the final scheduled contest for that week is complete.

3. Making Your Picks

For every eligible game, a player may select:

The home team
The visiting team
No team
Players may select any number of games from zero through every eligible game that week.

There is no requirement to make a minimum number of picks.

Your goal is to select a collection of games in which you believe you can go perfect.

4. Pick Locking

There are two different types of locks.

Individual Game Lock

Once an NFL game kicks off, that game is locked.

If you have not selected either team before kickoff, you have effectively skipped that game.

You cannot make or change a selection for that game after kickoff.

Sunday Noon Lock

Players may continue changing their selections for games that have not yet started until:

12:00 PM Central Time on Sunday

At exactly 12:00 PM CT:

All remaining games are locked.

This includes:

Sunday afternoon games
Sunday evening/night games
Monday games
Monday Night Football
The Sunday noon lock exists because NFL Desperation is not simply about picking winners. It's about deciding how much risk you are willing to take without knowing what everyone else is doing.

5. Pick Visibility

This is one of the fundamental rules of NFL Desperation.

Before Sunday at Noon

Players cannot see which teams other players have selected for future games.

They can see:

How many games each player has selected

but not which teams they have selected.

This information remains hidden until the relevant game begins.

After a Game Kicks Off

Once an individual game begins, selections for that game become visible.

As the weekend progresses, players therefore gain progressively more information about the field.

This creates an intentional element of strategy and information asymmetry.

6. Weekly Scoring

NFL Desperation uses all-or-nothing scoring.

Your weekly score is based on the number of qualifying games you selected.

If every one of your qualifying selections is correct, you receive the corresponding triangular score:

The formula is:

The Catch

If even one of your qualifying picks is incorrect:

Your weekly score is 0.

For example:

You select five games.

Game 1 — Correct
Game 2 — Correct

Game 5 — Incorrect
Your score:

0 points

You don't get 10 points for the four games you got right.

That's the whole point.

7. Tied NFL Games

If an NFL game ends in a tie, that game is removed from qualification entirely.

It is treated as though it did not happen for purposes of NFL Desperation scoring.

A tied game:

Is not a win.
Is not a loss.
Does not count as a correct pick.
Does not count as an incorrect pick.
Does not count toward the number of qualifying picks.
Example

You selected six games.

One ends in a tie.

Your other five selections are correct.

Your score is based on five qualifying picks:

5 × 6 ÷ 2 = 15 points

The tied game simply disappears.

8. Postponed Games

If a game is postponed but is ultimately played within the same Desperation week, it remains part of that week's games.

Your original selection remains attached to the game.

If a game is postponed into a different Desperation week, the original game is removed from the original week's scoring and becomes a new game in the rescheduled week.

Any player selections from the original week do not carry over.

The rescheduled game is therefore a blank slate.

9. Suspended Games

A suspended game remains active.

The game remains part of its original Desperation week until it is:

Completed, or
Officially canceled.
If the game is completed, its final result determines whether players' selections were correct or incorrect.

If the game is officially canceled, it is removed from qualification.

10. Completing a Desperation Week

A Desperation week officially concludes when the last scheduled game for that week has concluded.

In most weeks, this will occur following Monday Night Football.

The system follows the actual NFL schedule rather than assuming every week ends on Monday.

Once the final game is complete:

Weekly scores are finalized.
Standings are updated.
The week becomes part of the season history. 11. Season Competition

NFL Desperation is primarily a regular-season cumulative competition.

Players accumulate weekly points throughout the regular season.

Your cumulative score is the sum of your weekly scores.

Because a perfect week can produce a large number of points while an imperfect week produces zero, the leaderboard can change dramatically from week to week.

12. The Grand Prize

The Grand Prize is awarded after the penultimate week of the NFL regular season.

The player with the highest cumulative Desperation score at that point is the:

NFL Desperation Grand Prize Champion

The Grand Prize is based on total season points, not the score from the penultimate week alone.

13. Ties for the Grand Prize

If multiple players are tied for first place after the penultimate week, the Grand Prize is split evenly among those players.

There is no tiebreaker.

14. The Week 18 Playoff

After the Grand Prize is awarded, the remaining prize competition moves to Week 18.

The players who finish 10th place or higher after the penultimate week qualify.

If players are tied for 10th place, all tied players qualify.

Therefore, the Week 18 field may contain more than 10 players.

Example

Final regular-season standings:

Player A
Player B
Player C
Player D
Both Player B and Player C qualify.

Player D does not.

15. Week 18 Scoring

At the beginning of the Week 18 playoff:

All previous cumulative scores are reset to zero.

Every qualifying player starts Week 18 equally.

Weekly scoring works exactly as it does during the regular season:

Players select NFL winners.
All qualifying selections must be correct.
One incorrect selection results in zero points.
The triangular scoring system remains in effect.
The highest Week 18 scores determine the remaining prize positions.

16. Week 18 Prize Ties

If players tie for a prize position, the applicable prize money is divided according to the position of the tie.

Two players tie for 2nd

The two players split the combined 2nd- and 3rd-place prizes evenly.

Three players tie for 3rd

The three players split the 3rd-place prize evenly.

The 2nd-place prize remains unaffected.

There are no tiebreakers.

17. Flash Prizes

Flash Prizes are special prizes awarded for individual Desperation weeks during the regular season.

The purpose is simple:

Keep everyone desperate.

A player who is far behind in the overall standings can still have something significant to play for on any given week.

The highest weekly scorer wins the Flash Prize.

If multiple players tie for the highest qualifying weekly score, the Flash Prize is split evenly among all tied players.

There is no tiebreaker.

Flash Prize Announcement

A Flash Prize must be announced before the first game of that Desperation week kicks off.

Once the first game begins:

The Flash Prize configuration is locked.

A Commissioner cannot wait to see how the week unfolds and then decide to add a Flash Prize.

Flash Prizes are not awarded during the Week 18 playoff.

18. Joining an Existing Season

A Commissioner may choose whether their league allows players to join after the regular season has begun.

If midseason registration is enabled:

A new player cannot enter during an active Desperation week.
They must wait until the current week has concluded.
They begin competing with the next Desperation week.
They receive no points for previous weeks.
They are eligible for Flash Prizes beginning with their first eligible week.

19. Prize Structure

For Prize Leagues, the Commissioner determines the prize structure using percentages of the prize pool.

For example:

1st Place — 50%
2nd Place — 25%
3rd Place — 15%
Flash Prize — 5% each
Two Flash Prizes
Total allocation:

100%

The actual dollar value of each prize is determined by the number of players who register and the prize contribution per player.

Players must be able to see the expected prize structure before registering and paying.

Prize Structure Lock

The prize structure becomes locked once registration begins.

The Commissioner cannot change the prize allocation after players have begun registering.

20. Entry Fees

The Commissioner establishes the entry fee for each Prize League.

The platform will clearly distinguish between:

Prize Pool Contribution

and

Platform / Payment Fees

Players will see the total amount they are required to pay before registering.

The exact platform fee structure will be established separately as part of the platform's payment and business model.
